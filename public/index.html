Roster Chorale

Turn any Bach chorale into parts for the band or orchestra you actually have: winds, brass, strings, mallets and percussion, voiced for your roster, in a key that keeps everyone in range, at the grade you pick.

Warming up the arranger…

Step 1 Your roster

Upload a class list, paste rows from a spreadsheet, or just type what you have. Messy names like “Bb Clar”, “Vln 2”, “Tbn” or “Snare” are fine. Nothing is stored on the server.

No roster handy? Download the sample lopsided band.

Commas, dashes, colons, “x” or one per line all work.

Step 2 Key, chorale and grade

One per instrument: every flute reads the same part (small groups still split if needed to cover all four lines). Split sections: sections divide across the lines they suit, as Flute 1/2, Clarinet 1/2/3, Violin 1/2; tubas, bari saxes and string basses split into two parts in octaves. Small groups (a tuba quartet, euphoniums and tubas, a horn and trombone choir) share the four lines by register and by players: 4 euphoniums + 4 tubas gives euphoniums soprano/alto and tubas tenor/bass.

The grade sets each instrument's range, which concert keys are allowed (flat keys for winds, sharp keys for strings; mixed groups get the best compromise), and how much the rhythm is simplified (grade ½–1: eighth notes merged into quarters; grade 2: sixteenths into eighths; grade 3: as written).
